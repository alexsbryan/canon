# What each word canon uses means, one line each: `canon help <word>`.
#
# Every verb is here, every plain word canon prints, and the record's own
# names a person still meets in `canon log` or in JSON. The record keeps
# those names; this file is how a reader gets from one to the other.
#
# A line is `term: definition`. A term never holds ": ", so `consent:Nd`
# is one. Lines starting with # are notes for whoever edits this file.

# The verbs
init: start a canon in this folder, with you in charge of it.
add: write a rule down; it is in force once its area passes it, and a proposal until then.
review: go through what is waiting on you, one area at a time.
ui: open canon as a page in your browser, on this machine only.
approve: say yes to a proposal, or support an action with --about.
object: say no to a proposal, with your reason, or object to an action with --about.
list: the rules in force now, and what is proposed.
why: where a rule came from, what it replaced, and why.
amend: change a rule's words, and say why; the record calls it `supersede`.
repeal: take a rule away with no replacement; the record calls it `retract`.
accept: keep two rules that conflict, on purpose, until a review date.
dismiss: say two rules only seem to conflict.
undo: take back an act, yours or one in an area you are in charge of.
log: every act in the record, oldest first, under the record's own names.
question: write down something the rules do not cover yet.
who: who is in charge of an area, and how rules pass and actions are judged there.
give: put someone in charge of an area, until a date if you name one; the record calls it `grant`.
withdraw: step back from an area you are in charge of, or take someone off one.
area: put a rule in an area; the old name of the verb is `scope`.
rulings: how actions are judged here; the record calls it `policy`.
ratification: how rules pass here, area by area: who has to say yes, and how many.
allot: say what this commons has to share, like rooms or tools.
allocation: say how a shared thing goes round, like a rotation.
pool: whose turn it is with a shared thing, on a date.
position: the old way to support or object to an action; `approve --about` and `object --about` do it now.
decide: write down a ruling the group made about an action.
rank: mark a rule constitutional (`principle`), so it is harder to change, or ordinary (`convention`).
revisit: set a review date on a rule or an act; the record calls it `horizon`.
overdue: what is past its review date.
draw: pick people by lot, in a way nobody can steer.
silence: write down that something is left unwritten on purpose.
voice: what someone raised, and what came of it.
second: add your weight to an open question, without your name.
replay: decide this canon's own past again, to see what another way of judging would have done.
open: the questions nobody has answered yet.
witness: check the record against git: nothing taken out, nothing backdated.
guard: what protects the record from being edited behind canon's back.
identify: tie your name to your SSH key, so only you can write as you.
mcp: let an AI agent read the rules and ask canon, over stdio.
share: print a block another group can paste to copy your rules.
adopt: copy another group's rules to start from.
diff: how your rules differ from the ones you copied.
upgrade: take the updates from the group you copied rules from.
merge-driver: help git merge two copies of the record; run it once to set it up.
conflicts: rules of yours that conflict with each other; the old name of the verb is `tensions`.
config: what this canon is set up with, like which model reads for it.
draft: read your notes or documents and propose the rules already in them.
check: ask whether something you want to do fits the rules, and what to do next.

# Flags whose names changed, new and old
--area: the area a command is about, like `--area house.kitchen`.
--scope: the old name of `--area`.
--until: the date someone stops being in charge (`canon give`); `canon overdue` says when it passes.
--horizon: the old name of `--until`.
--constitutional: the ranks that are harder to change, like `principle` (`canon rulings set`, `canon replay`).
--entrench: the old name of `--constitutional`.
--escalate: the steps a repeat goes through, firmer each time, like `ask-one,ask-panel,refuse`.
--graduated: the old name of `--escalate`.
--rulings: another way of judging actions, for `canon replay` to try on your past.
--policy: the old name of `--rulings`.
--passes: another way of passing rules, for `canon replay` to try on your past.
--ratification: the old name of `--passes`.

# The plain words canon prints
rule: something the group agreed, in force until it is amended or repealed.
proposal: a rule someone wrote that has not passed yet.
area: a part of the canon with its own people in charge, like `house.kitchen`; a dotted path.
in charge: able to decide for an area: pass its rules, and give or take its charge.
in force: passed, and not amended or repealed since.
how rules pass here: what a proposal in an area needs before it is a rule; `canon ratification show`.
how actions are judged here: what the rulings say an action needs; `canon rulings show`.
ruling: a decision the group made about one action.
constitutional: a rule ranked `principle`, harder to change than an ordinary one.
go ahead: the action fits the rules; nobody has to be asked.
ask first: ask the person in charge before you do it.
ask everyone: the group decides this one together.
not allowed: the rules say no.
review date: the day a rule or a charge should be looked at again.
past its review date: its review date has come and gone, and nobody has looked again.
keep both: two rules conflict and the group keeps both on purpose (`canon accept`).
not a real conflict: two rules only seem to clash (`canon dismiss`).
the rules you copied from: the group whose canon you started from with `canon adopt`.
left unwritten on purpose: a thing the group chose not to make a rule about (`canon silence`).
the closest area decides first: whoever is in charge nearest a thing decides it before anyone above.
consequences that escalate: the second time something happens the answer is firmer, and the third firmer again.
must pass twice: a rule that needs two votes, with time or new people in between.

# How rules can pass (`canon ratification set <rule>`)
standing: whoever is in charge of the area says yes, and it is a rule.
joint: every one of the named people has to say yes.
threshold: so many yes votes pass it, and so many no votes stop it.
majority: more than half of those in charge say yes.
unanimous: everyone in charge says yes, and one no stops it.
share: that share of those in charge, like `share:60%` or `share:2/3`.
consent: it passes unless someone in charge objects within that many days, like `consent:14d`.
twice: it must pass twice, with days or someone new in between.

# How actions can be judged (`canon rulings set <rule>`)
default: the rules decide, and a person settles a conflict or a gap.
supermajority: a proposal carries when that share of the votes are for it.
cautious: what cannot be undone is never decided by silence.

# What `check` says to do
act: go ahead.
act-and-notify: go ahead, and tell the group.
ask-one: ask the person in charge first.
ask-panel: ask everyone, or a panel drawn for it.
refuse: not allowed.

# The record's own words, met in `canon log` and in JSON
commitment: the record's word for a rule.
assert: the record's name for writing a rule down (`canon add`).
supersede: the record's name for `amend`: a rule's words changed.
retract: the record's name for `repeal`: a rule taken away.
scope: the record's word for an area.
scoped: the record's name for putting a rule in an area.
grant: the record's name for `give`: someone put in charge of an area.
standing over: the record's way of saying someone is in charge of an area.
holder: the record's word for someone in charge.
policy: the record's word for the rulings: how actions are judged here.
ratify: the record's name for choosing how rules pass in an area.
ratification rule: the record's words for how rules pass here.
decided: the record's name for a ruling written down (`canon decide`).
graduated: the record's word for consequences that escalate for repeats.
subsidiarity: the record's word for: the closest area decides first.
entrenched: the record's word for constitutional rules being harder to change.
principle: a rule ranked constitutional: harder to change.
convention: an ordinary rule, changed the ordinary way.
horizon: the record's word for a review date.
tension: the record's word for two rules that conflict.
tensions: the old name of `canon conflicts`.
lineage: the record's word for the rules you copied from.
upstream: the group whose rules you copied.
generation: one version of the rules you copied, as their group published it.
provenance: where a rule came from: who wrote it, citing what.
adjudicate: to rule on whether an action fits the rules.
revert: the record's name for `canon undo`.
act: one line of the record: something somebody did, with who and when.
actor: who did an act: `human:<name>` for a person, `agent:<name>` for software.
profile: what kind of canon this is: `personal`, `code` or `house`.
endpoint: the address of the model `check` and `draft` ask.
