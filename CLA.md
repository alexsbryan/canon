# Contributor License Agreement

canon uses the [Harmony Contributor Agreements](https://www.harmonyagreements.org/)
(version 1.0), a standard, off-the-shelf set of contributor agreements. There
are two: an **Individual** agreement for people contributing on their own
behalf, and an **Entity** agreement for contributions made on behalf of a
company. Both appear in full below.

You sign electronically the first time you open a pull request: a bot posts a
one-time link, you confirm, and it records your signature against your GitHub
account. You only sign once; the same signature covers all your future
contributions.

## Plain-language summary (not part of the agreement)

This summary is a convenience, not a legal document. If it conflicts with the
agreements below, the agreements control.

- **You keep your copyright.** This is a license, not an assignment. You can
  still use, relicense, or sell your own contribution however you like
  (Section 2.1(a)).
- **You grant a broad license** to Alex Bryan, the maintainer, including the
  right to sublicense and relicense your contribution. This is what lets the
  project be offered under more than one license, for example a paid commercial
  license alongside the public AGPL one (Section 2.1(b), 2.2).
- **The project stays open source, permanently.** In return for that broad
  grant, the maintainer promises to always also make your contribution
  available under the license the project uses on the day you submit it
  (AGPL-3.0-or-later today) or another OSI-approved open-source license. The
  project cannot take your contribution closed-source-only (Section 2.3).
- **Everyone signs the same document.** There are no side deals and no special
  early-contributor terms. The rights you grant are identical to the rights
  every other contributor grants.
- **You confirm the work is yours to give** and that granting these rights does
  not break a promise you made to someone else, such as an employer
  (Section 3).

If you contribute as part of your job, your employer may own your work. In that
case use the **Entity** agreement below, or have your employer sign it, so the
grant is valid.
